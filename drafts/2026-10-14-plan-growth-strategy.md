Internal planning document. Not customer-facing.

## Crucial steps, in priority order

1. **Write down the proof.** Years on the statewide contract, units delivered per year, agencies served, three customers per segment willing to be named. Pull from billing; five calls to friendly customers. Unblocks every claim in every piece of marketing.
2. **Nail down SWC 209.** Contract term, exact eligibility language, allowed advertising wording, from the Central Procurement Office. The core differentiator; today the eligibility list cannot go on a flyer.
3. **Finish the county playbook as a working list.** All 95 counties plus cities and utility districts: gatekeeper, fiscal year, formal vs informal procurement, fleet spend from the budget book, brand mix. Researcher agent builds the first pass; a rep confirms before contact.
4. **Make the buying experience visible.** Six-step strip, standard quote, Ford Pro order status updates, delivery sheet, PO-matched invoice: put them on a fleet page on the dealership site and in every proposal.
5. **Own four to six events a year.** Sheriffs and chiefs are covered. Add county officials association, municipal league, utility districts, school boards, fire chiefs. Same three-touch pattern each: pre-event note, booth handout, follow-up within a week.
6. **Buy stock on purpose, by segment.** Already true for police (35 inbound Police Interceptor Utilities). Extend to trucks, admin SUVs, vans, with a monthly availability note.
7. **Define service after the sale.** Priority scheduling, mobile service, warranty, parts. Open item in the brief; first question after price.
8. **Measure five numbers monthly.** Quote requests, meetings, POs, units, by segment and source.

## Differentiating products and services

Competitors sell trucks. Each of these sells the thing around the truck.

1. **Fleet Replacement Plan.** Free annual review for a county or city: vehicle list with age and mileage, three-to-five-year replacement schedule timed to their fiscal year and Ford order banks, one-page plan for the budget request. Positions the department as the advisor who shows up in January. Also forecasts next year's orders. Best fit: rural counties with one purchasing contact.
2. **Patrol-Ready Tiers.** Three standard upfit builds (patrol, supervisor, admin), each one line on a quote, upfit slots reserved as units land. Demo unit per tier. Pairs with pooled police stock.
3. **Approval Packet.** For metro and formal buyers: one PDF with contract reference, pricing page excerpt, quote mapped to contract lines, cooperative purchasing justification memo, board agenda item template, W-9, insurance certificate. Fifteen minutes for a finance director instead of a week of chasing.
4. **Order Tracker.** One page per order showing stage (ordered, built, in transit, at upfitter, ready) from Ford Pro order data, with quote, PO, delivery sheet and invoice attached. Stages only, never dates. Turns "you know where your vehicle is" into a product.
5. **Funding Help.** Quarterly note on grants and funding for public fleets (homeland security equipment grants, USDA community facilities for rural counties, state programs), plus quotes formatted for grant applications.

## Recommended starting point

Steps 1, 2 and 3 first; they unblock everything else. Services 1 and 3 first: the replacement plan needs only a rep and a spreadsheet and feeds the pipeline a year out; the approval packet is documents the department already has, assembled once, and directly serves the "compliance, documentation, defined process" message for metro buyers.
